# Crisis — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Crisis (the 2014 strategy game by Scott Almes) — no content specifics. Useful as a checklist for designing resource-economy games with worker placement and negotiation.

## Shared Prosperity vs. Personal Gain
- The city (communal board) is a shared economy all players feed
- Players are also businesses pursuing private profit
- Tension between growing the common good and winning personally

## Worker Placement
- Players assign workers to city actions (jobs, projects, contracts)
- Action spaces are contested — placement order and blocking matter
- Worker count is a scarce resource to grow over time

## Resource Economy
- Goods are produced, refined, and sold through player-set prices
- Market prices are player-driven, not fixed — negotiation emerges
- Bankruptcy and scarcity pressure force difficult trades

## City Development Layer
- The city board upgrades and unlocks actions as it grows
- Public works grant benefits but cost private resources
- City health (stability/security) acts as a shared constraint

## Negotiation & Player Interaction
- Trading and price-setting are explicit mechanisms
- Deals are binding promises negotiated at the table
- Player count affects the negotiation dynamic (2–4)

## Win Condition
- Private wealth (money/profit) wins — not city prosperity
- Rounds proceed to a fixed end; final scoring compares private gain
