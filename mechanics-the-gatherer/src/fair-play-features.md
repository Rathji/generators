# Fair Play — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Fair Play (the 1996 auction game by Wolfgang Kramer) — no content specifics. Useful as a checklist for designing auction-based games with balance mechanics.

## Auction Core
- Players bid on items/services offered each round
- The auctioneer role rotates or is itself auctioned
- Bidding currency is limited — every bid is a commitment

## Balance & Fairness Mechanic
- A balancing mechanism redistributes advantage to weaker players
- The game's namesake feature: correcting unfair positions
- Balance tokens or compensation keep the game competitive

## Resource & Income Management
- Players earn income that funds future bids
- Overspending and under-spending both carry risks
- Income timing interacts with auction rotation

## Turn & Round Structure
- Rounds of simultaneous or sequential auctioning
- The auctioneer position changes each round
- Game ends after a fixed number of rounds

## Negotiation Layer
- Bidding is public; players react to each other's commitment
- Collusion and deal-making emerge naturally
- Player count (2–4+) changes the auction dynamics

## Win Condition
- Most valuable portfolio of won items wins at game end
- The balance mechanic keeps the late game tense
- Scoring is public and trackable throughout
