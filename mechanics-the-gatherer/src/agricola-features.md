# Agricola — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Agricola's features (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing worker-placement / farming-economy board games.

## Worker Placement Core
- Limited family members (workers); placing one claims an action space for the turn
- Most action spaces allow only one worker (direct blocking/competition)
- New action spaces unlock progressively as the game's round track advances
- Family growth: expanding your worker pool (and house) is itself a strategic cost

## Action Spaces
- Resource gathering spaces (multiple resource types, some fixed, some escalating)
- Field plowing, crop sowing, and harvesting actions
- Animal acquisition and husbandry actions
- Building/renovation actions
- Food-gain actions
- Card-play spaces (occupations and improvements)
- Family-growth spaces

## Farming Loop
- Field → plow → sow → grow → harvest cycle
- Multiple crop types with planting/growth timing
- Harvest events recur across the game, tying timing to the round structure

## Animal Husbandry
- Multiple animal species with breeding mechanics (pairs breed)
- Fenced pastures and stables house animals
- Animal surplus without housing loses value
- Animal count is a scoring factor

## Feeding Pressure
- Every family member must be fed at each harvest (food cost)
- Food economy: crops, animals, ovens/hearths, food-gain actions
- Shortfalls force begging cards (negative points)
- Sustained pressure drives the whole strategy space

## Cards (Occupations & Improvements)
- Personal hand of occupations (active abilities) and minor improvements (modifiers)
- Major improvements (hearths, ovens) enable food conversion chains
- Card synergy and hand management shape specialization

## House & Building
- House renovation tiers (wood → clay → stone)
- Adding rooms enables family growth
- Room count ties directly to worker count

## Round & Game Structure
- Fixed 14-round track; available actions expand as rounds progress
- Multiple harvests across the game
- Game end → final scoring across a large scoring matrix

## Scoring & Victory
- Points across many dimensions: fields, pastures, animals, fences, rooms, improvements, occupations, food, and cards
- Penalties for missing categories (empty spaces lose points)
- Well-rounded play vs. specialization tradeoff

## Modes
- 1–5 players; solo variant with its own scoring/benchmarks
- Optional card-drafting setup for fairness/variety
