# Suburbia — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Suburbia's features (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing city-building games with tile drafting and adjacency scoring.

## City Building Core
- Players build a personal city from a shared market of tiles
- Tiles are residential, commercial, industrial, or civic
- The city grows one tile per turn; layout defines scoring

## Tile Drafting Market
- A rotating market offers tiles that change price as demand rises
- Buying a tile often triggers a price bump for similar ones
- Drafting order and market manipulation matter

## Adjacency Effects
- Tiles score based on neighbors (population, income, reputation)
- Compatible zones boost each other; some zones clash
- Placement planning is the core spatial puzzle

## Three Resource Tracks
- Population (end-game points), income (buying power), reputation (prices)
- Tracks rise and fall with tile effects
- Balancing the three tracks against growth is the strategy

## Public vs. Private Goals
- Public goals award end-game points for city-wide achievements
- Secret goals give personal objectives
- Goal timing shapes which tiles you chase

## Endgame
- The game ends when the market empties or a score threshold hits
- Highest population total wins
- Scales 1–4 players; solo mode exists
