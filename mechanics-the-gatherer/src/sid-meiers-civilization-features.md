# Sid Meier's Civilization: The Boardgame — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Sid Meier's Civilization: The Boardgame's features (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing civilization-building games with parallel tech trees.

## Civilization Core Loop
- Players grow a civilization from a single settlement to a contender
- Turn phases: build, research, move/act, and upkeep
- Resource income (production, trade, culture, science) feeds every action

## Map Exploration
- Hex map explored by movement; tiles reveal terrain, resources, and ruins
- Expansion tension: more territory vs. longer supply lines
- Barbarian camps and neutral city-states populate the map

## Tech Tracks
- Four parallel tech tracks (e.g. military, science, economic, culture)
- Tech cards advance a track and unlock buildings, units, and bonuses
- Deep vs. wide research choices (specialize one track or balance several)

## City Management
- Cities founded on claimed territory; each city has a limit on improvements
- Buildings boost specific outputs (trade, defense, growth)
- City size/growth as a pacing resource

## Military & Combat
- Units move, attack, and defend with dice-based resolution
- Terrain and fortification modifiers
- Barbarian pressure forces military investment
- City conquest and razing as strategic options

## Economy & Trade
- Trade goods generated and spent on tech and buildings
- Trade routes and resource allocation between cities
- Money vs. military vs. research opportunity cost

## Events & Randomness
- Event deck delivers random world events each turn
- Events interact with cities, armies, and diplomacy

## Victory Conditions
- Multiple paths: reach the top of a tech track, military domination, or cultural/economic supremacy
- Track position doubles as scoring; endgame scoring layer
- Player count scaling (2–4)
