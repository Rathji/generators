# Paleo — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Paleo's features (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing cooperative survival games with hidden information and shared objectives.

## Cooperative Survival Core
- Players are a prehistoric tribe working together to survive
- The tribe must feed itself, build, and fend off dangers
- Everyone wins or loses together — no individual scoring

## Day/Night Turn Structure
- Day phase: players scout for resources and face events
- Night phase: the tribe resolves food needs and builds improvements
- Failing to feed the tribe has shared consequences (injuries, losses)

## Card-Based Discovery
- The deck is the world: cards represent locations, prey, resources, and perils
- Card backs hide what you'll encounter until committed
- Player skill is in risk assessment under partial information

## Task Management & Coordination
- Limited player time/actions each day forces specialization
- Some cards require specific skills or multiple players to resolve
- Communication shapes decisions; the tribe pools knowledge

## The Tribe's Shared Progression
- Earned resources build the camp (improvements, tools, structures)
- Knowledge/tech unlocks new card regions and better survival odds
- The tribe's condition (health, food stores) is a shared state

## Asymmetric Player Roles
- Players control a tribe member with a small personal deck
- Personal decks evolve as the game progresses
- Roles enable division of labor (hunter, gatherer, crafter)

## Danger & Failure States
- Encounters can wound or kill tribe members
- Deaths are permanent within the session — the tribe must adapt
- The shared failure condition creates pressure and teamwork
