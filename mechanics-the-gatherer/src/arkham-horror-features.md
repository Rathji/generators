# Arkham Horror (2nd Edition) — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Fantasy Flight's Arkham Horror 2nd edition (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing cooperative investigation / horror board games.

## Cooperative Investigation Core
- Fully cooperative play (1–8 players), all players win or lose together
- Investigator characters with multiple stats (combat, speed, sanity, stamina, lore, luck, etc.)
- Character asymmetry via unique abilities and stat spreads

## Board & Movement
- Map of connected city locations with special encounters
- Movement phases across the city and into other worlds
- Location-based encounters (city encounters vs. otherworld encounters)

## Mythos Phase / Turn Structure
- Fixed turn sequence: upkeep → movement → encounter → mythos
- Mythos phase drives the game: new events, clues, gates, and monster spawns each round
- Random mythos/environmental events escalate over time

## Gates & Other Worlds
- Portals open at locations; investigators enter otherworlds
- Encounter system for otherworld travel (progress toward closing gates)
- Gate sealing/closure mechanics (permanent sealing)

## Clues & Skill Checks
- Clue tokens placed by mythos events and found via encounters
- Skill checks: rolling dice pools against targets, spending clues to boost
- Check outcome tiers affect encounter results

## Monsters & Combat
- Monster spawn and movement on the board
- Evade vs. fight resolution
- Combat with weapons and spells
- Monster defeat rewards (trophies) usable for upgrades/recruitment

## Horror & Health
- Sanity and stamina damage tracks
- Going insane / being knocked unconscious (temporary setbacks)
- Character death/elimination with replacement options

## Items & Progression
- Item economy: weapons, equipment, artifacts, allies
- Spell system (casting with cost)
- Shopping/shop phase for purchases
- Trophies spent on training/recruitment

## Doom & Endgame
- Doom track advancing toward the ancient one's awakening
- Final battle against the ancient one
- Alternate win/loss conditions (seal enough gates vs. defeat the ancient one)
- Difficulty scaling with player count (gate bursts, monster surges)

## Modularity
- Expansions add boards, investigators, monsters, and rule modules
- Setup variability via randomized ancient ones and location decks
