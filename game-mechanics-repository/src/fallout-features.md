# Fallout: The Board Game — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Fantasy Flight's Fallout board game (no lore/content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing narrative adventure / exploration board games.

## Narrative Campaign Structure
- Scenario-based play: each session runs a scenario drawn from a campaign book; scenarios chain into a multi-session campaign
- Player goals: each player holds a secret personal quest PLUS shared cooperative objectives
- Win conditions tied to completing main objectives (cooperative outcome: all win or all lose together)
- Multiple play modes: solo, cooperative, and competitive (players can sabotage rivals)

## Wasteland Exploration
- Map of connected locations (board is split into named regions with routes between them)
- Exploration trigger: entering an unexplored location draws from an exploration deck
- Modular/random encounter deck (events, loot, enemies)
- Movement economy (limited movement points per turn)

## Dice Resolution System
- Character attributes (SPECIAL-style stats: Strength, Perception, Endurance, Charisma, Intelligence, Agility, Luck)
- Skill checks resolved by rolling dice against a stat target
- Outcomes scale with success count (marginal to strong successes)
- Luck/advantage modifiers that add dice or rerolls

## Combat
- Turn-based skirmish system (melee & ranged)
- Attack and defense dice; armor mitigation
- Weapon/armor item slots and hands limit
- Health system with damage, and lethality consequences

## Enemies & AI
- Hostile creatures and faction units controlled by simple AI behavior rules
- Enemy activation patterns (patrol/attack objectives logic)
- Faction-controlled territories that respond to player actions
- Reputation/alignment shift with factions based on your deeds

## Factions & Reputation
- Multiple competing factions; your standing with each changes over time
- Faction missions, favors, and consequences for siding with one over another
- Player actions can flip regions or trigger faction retaliation

## Quests
- Layered quests: personal, main story, and side quests
- Quest cards that advance story beats and gate content
- Agenda/narrative cards driving events between turns

## Character Progression
- XP gain → level ups → perk selection
- Spend points to improve base stats
- Recruitable companions/allies that accompany and assist
- Specialization builds (combat, speech, survival, tech)

## Resources & Items
- Caps as currency; junk and loot as tradeable resource
- Item acquisition and loadout management (limited slots)
- Consumables (chems/meds) with temporary effects
- Radiation and environmental hazard trackers

## Player Interaction
- Trading between players (gifting/trading items and caps)
- Shared board state: creatures, events, and objectives affect everyone
- Turn order and player-count scaling (solo to 4 players)
