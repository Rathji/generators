# Charterstone — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Charterstone's features (no lore/content details — sealed boxes and story beats intentionally omitted). Useful as a checklist for designing legacy / campaign board games.

## Legacy Campaign Core
- Persistent campaign: a set number of sessions played on the same board, with permanent changes between games
- Sticker-based board modification (permanent placement of new rules, buildings, and effects)
- Sealed box/compartment unlocks triggered by in-game conditions (hidden content revealed over time)
- Cumulative victory-point scoring across the whole campaign (winner decided at campaign end, not per session)
- Rule cards that permanently add or modify rules as the campaign progresses
- Player count flexibility (2–6) with automa/ghost systems filling empty charters

## Worker Placement
- Limited workers per player placed onto building spaces to trigger actions
- Worker recall mechanic (placed workers must be recovered before reuse; timing creates tension)
- Worker acquisition and upgrades over the course of the campaign
- Placement on own vs. other players' buildings (some trigger shared/public benefits)

## Buildings & Village Building
- Shared building deck; players claim and place buildings on a village grid
- Each player has a personal charter/mat with building sites
- Building upgrade paths (enhancement over time)
- Public/shared buildings that benefit multiple players when visited
- Building placement permanently changes the shared village board

## Cards & Decks
- Hand of cards with effects (play for benefits)
- Card drafting/selection each turn
- Cards permanently added to shared decks as rewards or campaign progression
- Deck composition evolves over the campaign

## Resources & Economy
- Multiple currency/resource types
- Resource conversion chains (buildings/actions transform resources)
- Income/rewards tied to buildings, cards, and placement
- Buying into other players' charters (shared economic interaction)

## Player Progression
- Personal reputation track (ongoing benefits as it climbs)
- Foresight/pre-planning tokens
- Individual goals vs. shared/communal objectives
- Cumulative personal board evolution between sessions

## Turn & Round Structure
- Structured turn sequence (card play, worker placement, actions, recall)
- Round/phase boundaries that force recall and new placement
- End-of-game scoring + campaign-end scoring layers

## Replayability & Novelty
- Gradual rule emergence (rules change as boxes unlock)
- New components/mechanics introduced mid-campaign
- High asymmetry created by each player's unique village growth
