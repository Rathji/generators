# Power Grid — Feature Reference

A system-level reference of Power Grid's features (no content specifics). Useful as a checklist for designing auction / market / network-building economic board games.

## Auction System
- Power plant acquisition via open ascending (English) auction each round
- Only one plant bought per player per round
- Auction dynamics: market timing, overspending, and denial (forcing opponents to overpay)
- Bidding order determined by current player standings

## Resource Market
- Multiple commodity types (e.g. coal, oil, garbage, uranium) with plant-fuel compatibility
- Shared market with supply-based price tiers (scarcity drives price up)
- Players buy fuel for their plants each round
- Buying changes the shared price tier (market manipulation is a strategy)

## Network / City Building
- Map of cities connected by routes; building connections costs vary by terrain/route
- Players claim cities and extend their network over the map
- Connection economics: cheap early routes, expensive late-game expansions
- Network reach determines how many cities can be powered

## Power Plants
- Plant cards with fuel type, cost, and output capacity (cities powered)
- Market structure with current + future plant pools
- Plants enter and leave the market on a schedule (step system)
- Fuel capacity limits per plant

## Electricity & Income
- Each round, power plants generate electricity for up to their capacity in cities
- Income rises with number of cities powered (income track per player)
- Cost/benefit: paying fuel now for future income
- Over- vs. under-capacity decisions

## Turn Order
- Dynamic turn order: players with fewer built plants act earlier
- Turn order matters for auctions, market purchases, and route-building
- Leads/followers tradeoff each round

## Step / Phase System
- The game progresses through steps that reshape the market: more resources appear, cheaper plants are removed, new plants enter, and end conditions change
- Pacing escalates as the game moves toward its end condition

## Victory & Endgame
- Game ends when a player reaches a city-count threshold; remaining round finishes
- Winner determined by most cities powered, ties broken by remaining money
- Player count scaling adjusts map area, market size, and thresholds

## Turn Sequence (per round)
- Ordered phases: set turn order → auction plants → buy resources → build connections/cities → produce electricity → earn income
- Every phase interacts with the shared markets and the map
