Email remains a key communication tool for both business and personal use. According to studies, the number of email users worldwide continues to grow, with an estimated 4.37 billion users in 2023, and a predicted 4.73 billion users by 2026.

Outbound Email Security

Outbound email security refers to the strategies and technologies designed to oversee and control the emails sent from an organization. Its primary goal is to protect sensitive information from being unintentionally shared and to adhere to data protection laws. 

Solutions for outbound email security include various features such as encryption, authentication, data loss prevention (DLP), email recall, and auditing capabilities to guarantee that emails reach their designated recipients securely.

DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance) is a critical component in outbound email security, providing organizations with the ability to authenticate, monitor, and manage the legitimacy of emails sent from their domains. By preventing unauthorized use of a domain (spoofing), DMARC helps protect both the organization’s reputation and its recipients from phishing and other malicious activities.

Role in Outbound Email Security

Ensuring the security of outbound emails is crucial for preventing domain spoofing and maintaining the integrity of your organization. Without the implementation of DMARC, malicious actors can easily forge emails to make them look like they originate from a trusted source, which is a common strategy used in phishing scams. By adopting DMARC with a "reject" policy, organizations can direct receiving servers to dismiss any unauthorized emails, significantly lowering the risk of their domains being exploited for fraudulent purposes.

  • DMARC Policies and Implementation

    DMARC offers three policies:

    • None: Monitors email without enforcing actions; useful for testing.
    • Quarantine: Sends unauthorized emails to the spam folder.
    • Reject: Blocks unauthorised emails altogether.

    Implementing DMARC involves:

    • Publishing a DMARC Record: The DMARC record, a DNS TXT entry (CNAME entry if Managed DMARC solution is activated), specifies the policy for how unauthenticated emails should be handled and provides a reporting mechanism.
    • Monitoring Reports: DMARC provides aggregate and forensic reports, allowing administrators to monitor who is sending emails on behalf of the domain and to track unauthorized sources.

Benefits of DMARC and Outbound Email Security

  • Reduced Phishing Attacks: With DMARC, phishing attempts using an organization’s domain are less likely to reach recipients, improving both security and brand trust.
  • Enhanced Visibility: DMARC reports give insights into email traffic, helping identify potential issues in email systems and services that are sent on behalf of the domain.
  • Improved Email Deliverability: Authentic emails are more likely to reach inboxes, as recipient servers trust the domain when DMARC is correctly configured.

Challenges

Implementing DMARC isn’t without challenges, especially for organizations with complex email infrastructures involving multiple third-party services. SPF has a 10-DNS-lookup limit, which can cause issues for organizations with multiple email vendors. Additionally, achieving DMARC alignment requires all sources to pass either SPF or DKIM or both protocols, which may necessitate technical adjustments.

DMARC is essential for outbound email security, ensuring only authorized sources can send emails from an organization’s domain, which helps prevent phishing and domain spoofing. By aligning with SPF and DKIM, DMARC improves deliverability, builds brand trust, and protects recipients from fraud. When combined with protocols like MTA-STS for secure transmission (inbound email security) and BIMI for visual brand authentication, DMARC forms part of a comprehensive email security strategy, reinforcing both reputation and resilience against email-based threats.
