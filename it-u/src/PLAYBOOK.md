# IT-U — Playbook

> Generated by `playbookToMarkdown()` in `src/framework/playbook.js` — do not edit by hand. Regenerate after changing that file.

This is the contributor and operator playbook for IT-U: the content model the
app implements, the conventions every record follows, how deployment
documentation is assembled, the publication envelope and its redaction rules,
and the checklists for adding a new asset type, template, service runbook or
export. The in-app copy lives under **Settings → Playbook & reference**.

## 1. The information model

Every entity record belongs to one of four information models, and (except a
relationship link) must be classified before it can be saved:

- **Core Asset** (`core-asset`) — A standardized asset type whose shape is fixed by IT-U and never reshaped by the user.
  Record types: `organizations`, `locations`, `contacts`, `configurations`, `passwords`, `trackers`, `domains`, `certificates`
- **Flexible Asset** (`flexible-asset`) — A customizable structured record defined by a Flexible Asset template.
  Record types: `flexibleAssets`
- **Document** (`document`) — Long-form authoring: documents, SOPs, checklists, site summaries and deployment runbooks.
  Record types: `documents`, `checklists`, `sites`, `diagrams`, `runbooks`
- **Integration-managed** (`integration`) — A record kept in step by an external system of record (PSA/RMM/ISP/voice platform).
  Record types: `flexibleAssets`, `configurations`, `domains`, `certificates`

## 2. Provenance classifications

Where a record's content came from and how it is kept. A provenance may require
extra origin detail on save:

- **Directly authored** (`authored`) — Written directly in IT-U by a person.
- **Imported once** (`imported`) — Imported from an external source once, and maintained here thereafter. Requires: `source`.
- **Continuously synchronized** (`synchronized`) — Kept in step by an external system of record. Requires: `source`.
- **Related to another record** (`related`) — Derived from, or reflecting, another record it links to. Requires: `sourceId`.
- **Linked to an external authoritative source** (`external`) — Points at an authoritative external source (a live URL). Requires: `sourceUrl`.
- **Stored as an attachment/document** (`attachment`) — Stored as a file or attachment rather than structured fields. Requires: `attachmentUrl`.

## 3. Relationship conventions

- **Express a fact once, as a link.** Relationship records are first-class typed links stored in `records.relationships` as `{ id, type: "relationships", kind, from: { type, id }, to: { type, id } }`. Never re-type the same fact into two records — link them.
- **Every kind declares its endpoints.** `RELATIONSHIP_KINDS` in `relationships.js` lists, per kind, the collections a link may start from and point at. A link is refused unless both ends match a known kind, which is what keeps the graph queryable and the exports meaningful.
- **Read both directions, store one.** `relationsOf(record)` reports a record's links in both directions, so "server → its applications" and "application → its server" are the same single link read two ways.
- **One link, no duplicates, no self-links.** The engine refuses duplicate links and self-links, cascades a record's links away when it is deleted, and audits the whole graph (`checkIntegrity`) for dangling endpoints, unknown kinds and duplicates.
- **Labels for humans, kinds for code.** A Flexible Asset shares one record collection, so `assetRelations.js` declares labelled GROUPS (backed by one or more kinds) that the asset profile renders as "Servers & workstations", "Credentials", "Vendor" and so on.

The catalog currently declares **154 kinds** over 14 collections, validated, deduplicated and audited by `checkIntegrity`.

## 4. How deployment documentation is assembled

- **1. Model the service as a structured record** A VoIP/PBX deployment starts from a Voice/PBX service record; a resold internet service starts from an Internet/WAN circuit record. Both are Flexible Assets with a curated field set (platform, transport, capacity, addressing, SLA) plus relationship buckets for the configurations, credentials, vendor, licensing and people around them.
  Files: `src/framework/voice.js`, `src/framework/circuit.js`, `src/framework/assetLibrary.js`
- **2. Assemble the deployment runbook** `RUNBOOK_TYPES` declares the runbooks (grouped VoIP / Internet / General) and `generateVoipRunbook` seeds a full draft from the service record — section by section — so a technician who did not design the deployment can execute it. Each runbook carries revisions and a review status.
  Files: `src/framework/runbook.js`
- **3. Pair it with a cutover checklist** `cutover.js` defines the phased pre-deployment / cutover / post-cutover checklist (port verification, test calls, failover, emergency-call test, rollback) with per-item completion, assignment and a recorded acceptance/sign-off.
  Files: `src/framework/cutover.js`
- **4. Cover the links and the lifecycle** `voipCoverage.js` (and the circuit equivalents) audits that every handset, credential, trunk, subscription, vendor and date is captured and linked, and the trackers/`lifecycle.js` keep the expiry and renewal dates visible.
  Files: `src/framework/voipCoverage.js`, `src/framework/lifecycle.js`
- **5. Publish it** `packet.js` renders the deployment packet (the runbooks, checklists and related records for one site/service) as markdown, HTML and a self-contained printable HTML; `publication.js` emits the machine-readable deployment bundle for the AI assistants and knowledge base. Both inherit the same default-deny redaction.
  Files: `src/framework/packet.js`, `src/framework/publication.js`

## 5. The publication envelope & redaction

- **One shared envelope.** `publication.js` defines `PUBLICATION_SCHEMA` (`itu-publication/1`, kind `itu-knowledge-bundle`) holding `records`, `relationships` and `sections`, so a downstream assistant reads a bundle without knowing anything about the store.
- **Default-deny credentials.** `redactRecord` OMITS credential records entirely unless the operator explicitly opts into a metadata-only summary; even then `secret` / `otpSecret` are never written and usernames are withheld unless requested. Embedded credentials stay out unless opted in.
- **Scrub prose.** `scrubText` removes PEM blocks, `otpauth://…secret=…` parameters and labelled secrets from free text, while leaving genuine cross-references ("password: see the credential record") alone. Private-key references on certificates are stripped.
- **Links only survive whole.** `redactForPublication` works on deep copies, keeps a relationship only when BOTH endpoints survive, and aggregates what was withheld by type and reason (never by name).
- **Refuse a leaky bundle.** `validatePublication` re-scans any envelope for secret fields and private-key material and REFUSES a leaky bundle whatever built it; `findLeakedSecrets` is the same scan the integrity suite runs on every export.

## 6. Extension playbooks

Work an extension top to bottom; each step names the file it touches. Run the
full test sweep and update this playbook's generator before declaring it done.

### A new asset type (Core Asset)

A Core Asset is a standardized record type: IT-U fixes its shape, the user fills in a form, and every export and audit knows the fields by name. Add one only for a concept the whole product should understand identically (a configuration, a domain, an SSL certificate, a runbook).

1. **Declare the collection.** Add the record type to `RECORD_TYPES` and give it a `RECORD_TYPE_META` entry (label, singular, icon). It is thereby classified (all types except `relationships` are).
   Files: `src/framework/docsets.js`
2. **Define the field schema.** Add a `<TYPE>_FIELDS` schema in its own vocabulary module and register it in `RECORD_FIELD_SCHEMAS`; `STANDARDIZED_TYPES` then includes it automatically.
   Files: `src/framework/standardized.js`
3. **Write the validation rule.** Add the type's branch to `validateRecordFields` (required fields, catalog membership) and an `*Issues(set)` audit that the Linter unions in.
   Files: `src/framework/standardized.js`, `src/framework/linter.js`
4. **Add relationships.** Add any new typed links to `RELATIONSHIP_KINDS` (declaring their `from`/`to` collections) so the type can be linked rather than duplicated.
   Files: `src/framework/relationships.js`
5. **Surface it.** The Assets station renders schema-driven forms via `asset-fields.js`/`asset-profile.js`; add a dedicated view only if the type needs bespoke interaction (as sites, diagrams and trackers do).
   Files: `src/modules/asset-fields.js`, `src/modules/asset-profile.js`
6. **Export & fixture it.** Confirm the type appears in the documentation bundle/packet inventory and add it to the integrity fixtures if it carries special rules.
   Files: `src/framework/publication.js`, `src/framework/packet.js`, `src/framework/fixtures.js`
7. **Test it.** Add a suite under `src/tests/` covering the schema, the required-field rule, the relationship kinds and a round-trip through the store.
   Files: `src/tests/docsets.test.js`
### A new template (Flexible Asset)

A Flexible Asset is a structured record shaped by a template from the ONE shared library (`flexibleTypes.js`), so a house standard is defined once and reused across every client. Most new asset concepts arriving in a phase should be a template, not a Core Asset.

1. **Author the template.** Add a `tpl(id, name, category, icon, description, references, fields)` entry to `BUILTIN_ASSET_TYPES`. Keep field keys stable — records, exports and tests depend on them; field types and options come from `flexible.js`.
   Files: `src/framework/assetLibrary.js`, `src/framework/flexible.js`
2. **Add any option catalogs.** If the template selects from a domain vocabulary (platforms, protocols, deployment models), add the `<domain>.js` options module rather than inlining labels in the template.
   Files: `src/framework/email.js`, `src/framework/network.js`, `src/framework/voice.js`
3. **Declare relationship groups.** Add a `RELATION_GROUPS["atype-…"]` entry so the asset profile shows labelled buckets (Servers, Credentials, Vendor, Licensing, Documents, People) instead of one flat link list; add any new kinds to `RELATIONSHIP_KINDS`.
   Files: `src/framework/assetRelations.js`, `src/framework/relationships.js`
4. **Wire the lifecycle.** Mark expiry-bearing fields `expiry: true` so `renewals.js`/`lifecycle.js` surface them on the renewals board and the expiry workflow.
   Files: `src/framework/renewals.js`, `src/framework/lifecycle.js`
5. **Add a coverage audit.** If the template is a *service* (email, backup, network, security, voice, circuit), add a `*Coverage.js` auditor that checks the client documented it fully — that it is what turns a pile of records into an audited deployment.
   Files: `src/framework/voipCoverage.js`, `src/framework/serviceAssets.js`
6. **Check the library.** The library re-adds any missing builtin on load (`restoreLibrary`); bump nothing by hand. Verify the template appears in the designer/library and can be cloned and reset.
   Files: `src/framework/flexibleTypes.js`, `src/modules/templates-view.js`
7. **Test it.** Add a suite covering the template's fields/references, its relationship groups and its coverage audit; re-run the full sweep.
   Files: `src/tests/flexible.test.js`, `src/tests/structured-assets.test.js`
### A new service runbook

A runbook is the executable form of a service deployment — written so a technician who did not design it can run it. It is a Document whose sections, statuses and revisions are managed by `runbook.js`, paired with a cutover checklist and lifecycle coverage.

1. **Declare the runbook type.** Add a `RUNBOOK_TYPES` entry with its `group` (VoIP / Internet / General) and section list; reuse `VOIP_RUNBOOK_SECTIONS` as the pattern for a new group's sections.
   Files: `src/framework/runbook.js`
2. **Write the generator.** Add a `generate*Runbook({ … })` that seeds the draft section by section from the service record (platform, transport, addressing, failover, tests), so the draft is never empty.
   Files: `src/framework/runbook.js`
3. **Define the cutover checklist.** Add the phased checklist definition (pre-deployment / cutover / post-cutover), its acceptance and its sign-off, so the runbook has a matching execution record.
   Files: `src/framework/cutover.js`
4. **Cover the links & lifecycle.** Add or extend the `*Coverage.js` audit so every device, credential, trunk, subscription, vendor and date the runbook relies on is linked and lifecycle-aware.
   Files: `src/framework/voipCoverage.js`
5. **Make it issuable.** Confirm the runbook is included by `buildDeploymentBundle`/`buildDeploymentPacket` for its service/site scope and renders in the printable packet.
   Files: `src/framework/publication.js`, `src/framework/packet.js`
6. **Give it an editor.** The runbook editor modal (`runbook-view.js`) is generic over `RUNBOOK_TYPES`; verify the new type opens, edits, revises and reviews.
   Files: `src/modules/runbook-view.js`
7. **Test it.** Add a suite covering generator output, the cutover phases, coverage and the issues audit.
   Files: `src/tests/runbook.test.js`, `src/tests/cutover.test.js`
### A new export

An export is either a machine-readable publication bundle for the assistants/knowledge base, or a human-facing packet/printable for a client or field technician. Both are built on the same redaction so a new export cannot leak credentials.

1. **Declare the kind.** Add to `BUNDLE_TYPES` (publication.js) and/or `PACKET_KINDS` (packet.js) with a label and description; the existing builders switch on the kind.
   Files: `src/framework/publication.js`, `src/framework/packet.js`
2. **Assemble the content.** Add a `build*Bundle` / `build*Packet` that selects the records, relationships and sections for the kind, built on `redactForPublication` so it inherits default-deny.
   Files: `src/framework/publication.js`, `src/framework/packet.js`
3. **Render it.** Provide markdown, HTML, and (for a printable) a self-contained HTML via `packetToStandaloneHtml`, with a `packetFilename`; keep the contents list anchored to the renderer's heading ids.
   Files: `src/framework/packet.js`, `src/framework/markdown.js`
4. **Guard it.** Confirm `validatePublication` and the integrity suite's `exports` check cover the new kind, and that any new secret-bearing field is registered in `SECRET_FIELDS`.
   Files: `src/framework/publication.js`, `src/framework/password.js`, `src/framework/integrity.js`
5. **Log & surface it.** Add the kind to the Exports station (choose, preview, download, publish/host) and to the bounded export log so what was published when is recorded.
   Files: `src/modules/exports.js`
6. **Test it.** Add a suite proving the new export builds, renders, and REFUSES a leaky bundle; re-run the full sweep.
   Files: `src/tests/publication.test.js`, `src/tests/packet.test.js`
